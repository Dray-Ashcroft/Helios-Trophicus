Sunlight 100%→PAR <50%→Captured 2–10% of PAR
Of the sunlight reaching plants, less than half is photosynthetically active radiation (PAR, 400–700 nm). Plants capture only about 2–10% of the PAR in photosynthesis. That captured energy (GPP), 100 rel. J here, supports every level above.
GPP 100 − R 50 heat = NPP 50
GPP: energy fixed in photosynthesis. R: used in the producers' own respiration, lost as heat. NPP: stored as plant biomass — food for herbivores and decomposers.
NPP 50 → TL2 5 eaten + Litter 45
Herbivores can only eat what producers keep after respiration — the NPP, not the GPP. Only about 10% of it reaches TL2: 50 × 0.10 = 5. The other 90% (45) is not passed on: uneaten leaves, stems and roots become litter for the decomposers (step 6).
TL2 5→R 3 heat+Waste 1.5+TL3 0.5
The herbivores took in about 5. They use about 3 in their own respiration, lost as heat. About 1.5 leaves as faeces and dead bodies for the decomposers, and only about 0.5 (10%) reaches the secondary consumers. The other 90% is not transferred.
TL3 0.5→R 0.3 heat+Waste 0.15+TL4 0.05
The secondary consumers took in about 0.5. About 0.3 is respired and lost as heat. About 0.15 goes to the decomposers and only about 0.05 (10%) reaches the top carnivores: too little to support a fifth level. The 10% law is a generalisation: real transfers vary between ecosystems, commonly about 5–20%.
Dead matter ~47→Decomposers→Heat+Nutrients
Leaf litter (45) and the waste and dead bodies of every level (about 1.7) reach the soil. Fungi, bacteria and soil animals break them down; their respiration releases the remaining energy as heat. What is left is inorganic nutrients, such as nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium, in the soil.
Nutrients N · P · K→Roots→TL1 producers·Energy not recycled
The nutrients dissolve in the soil water. Producers absorb them through their roots and build them into new tissue. Matter cycles back to the producers, but energy does not: it flows one way and is lost as heat, so the ecosystem needs the Sun every day.
